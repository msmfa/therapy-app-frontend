# Therapy Reminder Schedules (Gap-Length Framework)

This document outlines evidence-based reminder schedules to help clients recall key insights from therapy sessions.
The framework adapts to the **actual gap length between sessions**, rather than fixed weekly/biweekly categories.
This approach is grounded in cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and psychotherapy research.

---

## Why Reminders Matter (Science Background)

- **Forgetting curve:** Without review, people forget a large portion of new material within days.
    - Ebbinghaus, H. (1885/1913). _Memory: A Contribution to Experimental Psychology._

- **Spacing effect:** Revisiting material at increasing intervals strengthens long-term retention.
    - Cepeda, N. J., et al. (2006). _Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis._ _Psychological Bulletin, 132_(3), 354–380.

- **Neuroplasticity:** Repeated activation of new experiences supports consolidation and structural brain changes.
    - Kandel, E. R. (2001). _The molecular biology of memory storage: A dialogue between genes and synapses._ _Science, 294_(5544), 1030–1038.

- **Therapy continuity:** Clients often forget 40–60% of session content within days without reinforcement. Reminders help maintain therapeutic momentum.
    - Kazantzis, N., et al. (2010). _Homework in cognitive behavioral therapy: A meta-analysis._ _Clinical Psychology: Science and Practice, 17_(2), 144–156.

## General Framework: Reminders by Gap Length

| Gap Length     | Suggested Reminders | Distribution                                                                      |
| -------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1–2 days**   | 1 reminder          | Same evening of session (captures consolidation window)                           |
| **3–6 days**   | 3 reminders         | Same evening • Midpoint (~50% into gap) • Pre-session                             |
| **7–14 days**  | 4 reminders         | Same evening • ~25–30% into gap • ~60–70% into gap • Pre-session                  |
| **21–30 days** | 5 reminders         | Same evening • ~3–4 days after • ~10–12 days after • ~20 days after • Pre-session |

**Rationale:**

- The **same-day reminder** is critical for encoding and early consolidation.
- The **midpoint reminders** counteract steep forgetting during the first week.
- The **pre-session reminder** refreshes memory for continuity going into the next therapy session.
- Longer gaps require more touchpoints to keep insights active in memory.

## Worked Examples

### Weekly Therapy (7-day gap)

- Monday evening: quick reminder
- Thursday (midpoint): refresher
- Sunday: pre-session cue
  ➡ Matches the **3–6 day gap schedule** (3 reminders).

---

### Biweekly Therapy (14-day gap)

- Day of session (evening)
- Day 3–4 (25% into gap)
- Day 7–8 (midpoint)
- Day 12–13 (pre-session)
  ➡ Matches the **7–14 day gap schedule** (4 reminders).

---

### Monthly Therapy (28–30 day gap)

- Day of session (evening)
- Day 3–4
- Day 10–12
- Day 20
- Day 28–29 (pre-session)
  ➡ Matches the **21–30 day gap schedule** (5 reminders).

---

### Mixed Frequency Example: Monday → Wednesday → Monday

- **Mon → Wed (2-day gap):** only same-day reminders (Mon & Wed evenings).
- **Wed → Mon (5-day gap):** same-day (Wed), midpoint (Fri/Sat), pre-session (Sun).
  ➡ Combines **1–2 day** and **3–6 day gap schedules**.

---

## Summary

- **Always anchor the first reminder on the same day** as the session.
- **Scale the number of reminders to the gap length** between sessions.
- **Use midpoint and pre-session touchpoints** to counter forgetting and ensure continuity.
- This flexible system adapts to irregular therapy schedules and aligns with how memory and neuroplasticity work.
